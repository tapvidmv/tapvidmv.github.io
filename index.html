TAPVid-MV: Tracking Any Point in 3D Across Multiple Views

The first benchmark for long-term 3D point tracking across several synchronized, moving cameras.

Overview

  • A task that no existing benchmark measures. Given synchronized videos from several moving cameras and one query pixel in one view, a method must predict that point's 3D trajectory in a shared world frame and its visibility in every view. Point-tracking benchmarks so far cover a single video or a static multi-camera rig; TAPVid-MV is the first to test long-term 3D tracking across several views under camera motion.
  • 284 sequences, 1,142 calibrated camera streams, 109,769 3D point tracks. Seven subsets span the domains where multi-camera capture already happens: robot manipulation (DROID), sporting activities (EgoExo4D), close-contact human interaction (Harmony4D), tabletop object rearrangement (PACE), two-person interaction (Hi4D), driving (Waymo), and procedurally generated indoor scenes (Perpetua). Each scene has three to eight cameras, every subset contains camera motion, and three include egocentric views.
  • Ground truth you can check yourself. Each domain is annotated from its most reliable signal — robot kinematics and stereo, fitted human meshes, posed object meshes, LiDAR, or exact synthetic geometry — then automatically filtered and manually inspected in every view. All 284 sequences are published as interactive 3D recordings, so any annotation can be verified without downloading the dataset.
  • An evaluation that separates geometry from correspondence. We score tracking in the query view, in the other views, and in world space, and we score the quality of each system's reconstruction on the same sequences. That tells you whether a method's error comes from the geometry it recovered or from the correspondences it predicted — the two are usually confounded.
  • More than 30 baselines under one protocol. Six method families, from monocular 2D trackers to joint multi-view 4D models, with every point tracker reading the same shared multi-view reconstruction so differences come from the tracker alone. No method comes close to solving the task, multi-view trackers do not consistently beat monocular ones, and geometry recovery is the dominant bottleneck.
  • Training data, and uses beyond this benchmark. We release the procedural Perpetua generator and metric 3D trajectories for 5,371 DROID robot episodes as multi-view training data. The annotations also support monocular 2D and 3D point tracking, future-trajectory prediction, and 4D reconstruction.
